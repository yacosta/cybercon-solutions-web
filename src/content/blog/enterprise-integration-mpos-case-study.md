Enterprise clients don’t buy “a roadmap.” They buy integrations that work on launch day, payment flows that clear PCI review, and mobile point-of-sale rollouts that don’t strand field teams. Before founding Cybercon Solutions, Yezid Acosta served as **CIO** of a mid-market technology delivery firm: multi-year platform planning, a **$2M+** delivery budget, and **19 developers** across onsite, remote, and offshore teams.

This is the operating pattern he ran.

## What he walked into

Growth plans outran the technology story. Client commitments needed platform integrations, security, and infrastructure sequenced against real delivery dates — not a wishlist. Payment and inspection workflows crossed North America and Europe, which meant **PCI** and **EMV** weren’t optional chapters at the end of a project plan.

That’s the daily reality in [professional services IT](/industries/legal-professional-services/): billable delivery, client security questionnaires, and engineering capacity that has to stay honest.

## Owning the multi-year technology roadmap

As CIO, he owned the multi-year technology roadmap — aligning platform integrations, security investment, and infrastructure to client growth plans and enterprise delivery commitments. Sequencing mattered: what unlocked the next client milestone, what reduced risk, and what could wait without becoming a hallway ambush.

Roadmaps fail when they’re product catalogs. His were decision tools.

## Large-scale integrations that had to work in production

He delivered large-scale enterprise integrations — including theme-park / resort operations workflows and mobile inspection software for a European industrial-inspection context — the kind of projects where “almost integrated” still means the client can’t open the gates or close the visit.

Integration work followed the same discipline Cybercon applies today in [IT consulting](/services/it-consulting/): clear interfaces, security review, and owners on both sides of every handoff.

## Engineering leadership across a blended team

Nineteen developers across onsite, remote, and offshore seats only works if someone owns priorities, quality, and the client story. He ran that blend against a $2M+ budget — so capacity matched commitments instead of hope.

## PCI, EMV, and mPOS without theater

He kept PCI/EMV compliance honest for domestic and European client work, oversaw network security and infrastructure, and ran end-to-end **mPOS** rollouts across North American enterprise customers. Payment projects fail when compliance is a slide and operations is an afterthought. He treated them as one delivery.

## What we’d tell another delivery-firm executive

Put one CIO-level owner on the roadmap or every client becomes a special snowflake stack. Budget security and payment compliance inside the project, not after sales closes. And treat offshore capacity as part of one team with one definition of done — or you’ll ship surprises.
