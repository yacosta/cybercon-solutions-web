Premium retail looks simple from the sales floor. Behind it: multi-country operations, boutique networks, regional vendors, and a corporate headquarters that wants one technology story — not six.

Before founding Cybercon Solutions, Yezid Acosta led North American technology for a global premium retail brand across **six countries** in the Americas — USA, Canada, Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, and Argentina — running US and Canadian operations across **50+ boutiques** and coordinating regional standards, vendors, and compliance.

Here’s how that leadership actually ran.

## What he walked into

Each country had its own habits. Boutiques needed working point-of-sale, connectivity, and support that didn’t wait for a ticket to cross three time zones. Corporate HQ needed a North American technology liaison who could speak operations and standards without translating every request into a crisis.

That’s classic [distribution, retail, and manufacturing IT](/industries/distribution-retail-manufacturing/) pressure: local reality, global expectations, and no patience for downtime during peak seasons.

## Running US and Canadian boutique operations

He ran IT operations for US and Canadian boutiques — the day-to-day of keeping stores online, vendors accountable, and local managers from inventing shadow workarounds when something broke.

Boutique IT fails in small ways that feel huge on a Saturday: a payment terminal, a circuit, a password nobody owns. The operating model had to be boring and reliable.

## Regional standards without erasing local reality

Across Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, and Argentina, he coordinated technology standards and vendor relationships so North America didn’t become six incompatible stacks. Standards covered what had to be common — identity patterns, security baselines, support escalation — while leaving room for local regulatory and carrier realities.

The goal wasn’t identical every country. It was interchangeable enough that HQ and regional leaders could plan.

## The HQ liaison role that prevents thrash

He served as the North American technology liaison to corporate headquarters in Europe. That meant packaging regional reality into decisions HQ could fund, and bringing global standards back without pretending every boutique was a headquarters office.

Without that role, every project becomes a negotiation restart.

## What we’d tell another retail executive

Pick a single owner for regional standards — or you’ll pay for the same integration six times. Staff boutique support like revenue depends on it, because it does. And give HQ a named liaison who can say no with data, not just escalate with emotion.

[Cloud](/services/cloud/) and [managed IT](/services/managed-it/) only help if the operating model already knows who owns a boutique outage at 2 p.m. on a launch weekend.

## How this experience shapes Cybercon’s approach

This is founder leadership experience — the North American retail technology leadership Yezid Acosta ran before founding Cybercon Solutions, not a Cybercon customer engagement. It’s the reason Cybercon designs multi-country retail IT the way it does today: North American operations for a boutique network, multi-country standards and vendor coordination, and a standing liaison bridge to global HQ, with local providers kept in the mix under one regional operating model instead of six separate IT departments that never talk.
